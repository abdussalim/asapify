ASAPify: early detection of smouldering peat

ASAPify detects fire that is already smouldering inside peat soil but has not yet burned the vegetation on the surface. Two geostationary satellites, Himawari-9 and GK2A, observe every peat pixel in Sumatra and Kalimantan (15 provinces) every 10 minutes through the night. A deterministic rule engine (MAUT + neighbour support) flags pixels as AWAS; only then does a Gemini agent fetch the latest VIIRS data and imagery to verify, and an operator decides before the Telegram bot posts to the alert group. AI is not the detector.

02 · Scope

Detect burning that has already started, not predict risk

✅ "Two-satellite observation + deterministic rules detect anomalies; neighbouring pixels validate them spatially; AWAS pixels are verified by AI with VIIRS and imagery; an operator decides; the Telegram bot posts to the alert group."

In scope ✅

  • Early detection of combustion: fire already present in the soil
  • Area: all peat in Sumatra + Kalimantan (15 provinces, codes 11–19, 21, 61–65; widened 5 Oct)
  • Peat pixels only (KHG peat mask, excluding built-up areas)
  • Detection: Himawari-9 + GK2A, at night, MAUT + neighbour support
  • Environment baseline: NASA POWER (TS as LST, T2M as SAT)
  • On-demand verification: VIIRS (FIRMS points + GIBS imagery) + Himawari imagery
  • Send decision by an operator (publish / reject)

Out of scope ✅

  • Risk prediction before burning (water table, rainfall, drought indices)
  • AI that detects or sets status
  • ToPeCAl and Landsat in the operational path (post-event roadmap only)
  • Periodic FIRMS/VIIRS ingest for the whole area
  • Operator-chosen Waspada/Siaga/Awas levels
  • Already-burned peat (NBR/dNBR, burned area); SiPongi hotspots; MODIS
  • Peat in Sabah, Sarawak, Brunei; field officers; two-way bot

04 · Why the 3.8–3.9 µm channel at night

The shortwave channel is about 25 times more sensitive to small embers

Planck calculation: the brightness-temperature shift of one 2 km pixel caused by a 400 °C ember on a 298 K background. Applies to Himawari B07 (3.9 µm) and GK2A SW038 (3.8 µm).

Ember area10.5–11.2 µm (B14 / IR105)3.8–3.9 µm (B07 / SW038)
90 m²0.02 K0.5 K
1,000 m²0.2 K5.4 K
1 ha2.1 K33 K
  • The 3.8–3.9 µm channel (mir) carries the main signal; the 10.5–11.2 µm window channel (win) is used for cloud, ΔLST and ΔSAT.
  • In daytime the 3.8–3.9 µm channel is mixed with reflected sunlight; at night it is clean. That is why evaluation runs at night only ✅.
  • Two satellites, two viewing angles: GK2A (128.2° E) sees Kalimantan and Sumatra more directly than Himawari-9 (140.7° E), so its pixels are smaller there. Noise or a navigation error on one satellite is not enough to trigger AWAS.
  • ⚠️ Pixels in Kalimantan are larger than 2 km (far from nadir), and larger still in Sumatra (especially for Himawari), so real sensitivity is lower than the table.
05 · Rule engine ✅ MAUT + neighbour support

Six attributes, one utility score, four statuses

✅ Mechanism: MAUT combines the two-satellite attributes, the environment baseline and time; neighbours validate spatially. 💡 The figures below are starting values calibrated in the backtest (A7, A8); changes are logged in WORKLOG section 7.

Formulas used in code (jobs/lib/maut.py)

z_s = (mir − median) / (1.4826 × max(MAD, 0.3 K)) z_rel_s = z_s − median(z_s of neighbours within 5 cells ≈ 10 km) u_H = ramp(z_rel Himawari; 2 → 4) u_G = ramp(z_rel GK2A; 2 → 4) u_LST = ramp(ΔLST; 1 K → 4 K) u_SAT = ramp(ΔSAT; 0 K → 3 K) u_T = min(k / 3, 1) U0 = Σ wᵢuᵢ / Σ wᵢ over the available H, G, LST, SAT, T u_N = #(neighbours U0 ≥ 0.5) / #(observed neighbours), 8 directions U = Σ wᵢuᵢ / Σ wᵢ over H, G, LST, SAT, T, N ramp(x; a → b) = clip((x − a) / (b − a), 0, 1)

Guardrails ✅

  • No status or level field in the output
  • strong_evidence when smoke is visible ≤ 5 km from the coordinate, or there is a VIIRS detection ≤ 2 km within 48 hours
  • Otherwise inconclusive. No visible smoke is never grounds for rejection
  • The validator rejects when viirs_within_2km_48h > 0 but the result is inconclusive, or when a number in the summary is not in the tool results
  • At most 8 tool calls; counted in the tool layer
Core agent instructionagent/agent.py
You verify AWAS thermal anomalies on Indonesian peatland.
The rule engine has already identified the coordinate as AWAS.
Your task is verification, not detection.
1. Read the candidate context (get_pixel_context).
2. Retrieve the latest VIIRS detections around the coordinate.
3. Retrieve the latest VIIRS true-colour image and a Himawari
   image of the same area.
4. Look for visible smoke or other visual evidence consistent
   with smouldering or fire activity.
5. Treat absence of visible smoke as inconclusive, never negative.
6. Never change the rule-engine status. Never invent numbers.
7. Save the result with save_verification.
ToPeCAl and Landsat ✅ removed from the EWS path

Landsat passes over a given location only once every few days, so it does not suit early warning, which needs high frequency. ToPeCAl (Sofan et al. 2019) is used neither for detection nor for verification.

It can still be mentioned as a roadmap: post-event analysis, dataset validation, research, model evaluation.

07 · Limitations

Stated plainly in the pitch

  • ✅ Detects only smouldering areas that are large enough; it is not an in-situ sensor.
  • ✅ Cloud over both satellites means missing data, not a safe signal.
  • ✅ MAUT weights and thresholds are calibrated on one season (2023) in one sub-area; they need recalibration per season and region.
  • ✅ NASA POWER is coarse (0.5° × 0.625°) and ~1.5 days late, so it is used only as a 30-day baseline, not as tonight's measurement.
  • ✅ VIIRS true-colour imagery exists only in daytime (~13:30) and is often cloudy; smouldering smoke is thin. No visible smoke = inconclusive.
  • ✅ VIIRS detections come from only ± 2 passes per satellite per day; no detection does not mean no fire.
  • ✅ The backtest starts in 2023 because the GK2A archive on NOAA open data begins on 16 Feb 2023.
  • ⚠️ Pixels in Kalimantan are larger than 2 km and larger still in Sumatra, so small embers go undetected.

16 · Glossary

Short dictionary

Smouldering
Low-temperature glowing combustion inside peat, with no flame at the surface.
Brightness temperature (BT)
Temperature computed from the radiance of one satellite channel, without emissivity correction.
mir / win
The 3.8–3.9 µm channel (Himawari B07, GK2A SW038) and the 10.5–11.2 µm window channel (B14, IR105).
GK2A
GEO-KOMPSAT-2A, Korea's (KMA) geostationary satellite at 128.2° E. The second, independent observation.
MAUT
Multi-Attribute Utility Theory: each attribute becomes a 0–1 utility, then a weighted sum gives one utility score.
Neighbour support (u_N)
Share of the 8 surrounding neighbours that are also anomalous. Filters out single-pixel anomalies.
LST / SAT
In ASAPify: TS (Earth Skin Temperature) and T2M from NASA POWER, used as a 30-day hourly baseline.
AWAS
The rule-engine status that triggers AI verification. Not a confirmed fire. The word is Indonesian ("alert") and is kept in both languages.
NO_OBSERVATION
Both satellites are under cloud or data is missing. It does not mean safe.
Cluster
Adjacent AWAS pixels. The agent is called once per cluster.
VIIRS / FIRMS / GIBS
A 375 m sensor on three polar satellites; FIRMS provides its detection points, GIBS its true-colour imagery. In ASAPify: on-demand verification evidence.
strong_evidence / inconclusive
The two possible agent results. There is no "negative" result.
ADK built-in loop
The agent calls tools repeatedly until the task is done or a limit is reached.
Schema-locked
Agent output must match the JSON schema; it cannot add other fields.
